Executive Rationale
Bridging the Digital Divide via Sub-2GHz FWA & LEO Backhaul
Slide 02 / 10

1. The Structural Terrestrial Fiber Divide

Terrestrial fiber trenching in rural Class 3 to 6 municipalities carries severe negative return on investment (ROI) for legacy telcos (PLDT, Globe, DITO). Prohibitive civil works across mountains, archipelagos, and agricultural valleys leave over 40,000 provincial barangays completely stranded without high-speed broadband.

2. Band n50 Sub-2GHz Propagation Advantage

TelTech's 80 MHz contiguous nationwide spectrum in Band n50 (1.5 GHz) provides a 3.5 km non-line-of-sight (NLOS) rural radius (~38.5 km² coverage area per macro tower). It punches through heavy tropical foliage, monsoonal rainfall, and concrete hollow block structures where 3.5 GHz (C-band) fails completely.

3. Zero-Trenching Rapid Deployment (Starlink LEO Backhaul)

Utilizing Starlink Business LEO satellite backhaul (220+ Mbps DL, 28–45 ms latency) enables fully functional macro cell deployment in 48 to 72 hours. This entirely bypasses 12 to 18 months of costly civil trenching, highway right-of-way (ROW) permits, and pole attachment negotiations.

4. The Dual-Revenue Commercial Cash Flow Engine

Every macro site captures DICT FPIAP institutional subsidies (₱15,000–₱25,000/mo per public school/barangay hall) as guaranteed government anchor revenue, while selling residential broadband at ₱599/mo to 600 households, generating a rapid payback of under 14 months per tower.
