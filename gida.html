Why Band n50 is the Ideal Carrier for Rural FWA

  • Propagation Superiority: At 1.5 GHz, signal attenuation is 9–12 dB lower than 3.5 GHz (n78). Delivers an effective cell radius of 2.0 km to 3.5 km in rural terrain (12–35 km² footprint), covering an entire barangay with 1 BTS.
  • Indoor Penetration: Easily penetrates typical Philippine residential building materials (GI sheet roofing, hollow blocks, timber) without requiring mandatory outdoor antenna roof-mounts for every home.
  • Massive 60 MHz Carrier Bandwidth: Yields 499 Mbps aggregate DL throughput, comfortably sustaining 1,000 active subscribers at 15–25 Mbps busy-hour bursts.

Selection Methodology
Site Selection Pillars: Necessity, Viability & Ease of Deployment
Slide 3 / 14
Pillar 1 · 30% Weight

1. Necessity

Lack of Internet Service
  • Wireline FTTH Deserts: Target rural barangay clusters where commercial FTTH, DSL, and cable broadband are completely absent.
  • Overcome Mobile Congestion: Relieve severe mobile cellular 4G/5G data congestion with dedicated fixed wireless home capacity.
  • Commercial Preemption: Capture unserved households before wireline telcos consider building out.
  • Metro Overbuild Avoided: Metropolitan NCR deprioritized due to saturated fiber price competition.
Pillar 2 · 35% Weight

2. Business Viability

Economic Activity to Generate Sales
  • Addressable Household Density: Focus on 3,000 to 12,000 population (700 to 2,800 HH), providing ideal subscriber scale for 1 BTS.
  • Purchasing Power: Prioritize 1st–4th class municipal trading centers with strong agricultural/commercial cash flow.
  • Retail Sales Velocity: Proven consumer ability to sustain ₱599 to ₱999/mo recurring subscriptions.
  • Rapid Payback: Fast customer take-up delivers full site CAPEX payback in 14 to 22 months.
Pillar 3 · 35% Weight

3. Ease of Deployment

Towers, RoW, Fiber & Power
  • Presence of Fiber: 100% of Batch 1 connects directly to Converge backbone nodes (<1–3 km drop), zero dark fiber civil works.
  • Grid Power Availability: Connected to commercial 220V grid via Meralco or provincial Electric Cooperatives (BENECO, CASURECO, etc.).
  • Tower Erection & Colocation: Co-locate on existing TowerCo structures (EDOTCO, EdgePoint) or rapid monopole erection.
  • Right-of-Way & Road Access: Highway/provincial road proximity ensures heavy crane access and zero land disputes.
MATHEMATICAL COMPOSITE SCORING: Score = (0.35 × Deployment) + (0.35 × Viability) + (0.30 × Necessity)

Phasing Strategy: 1 BTS per Priority Barangay

  • Maximum Geographic Footprint: Deploying 1 n50 BTS in each of the top 1,000 barangays reaches 271 municipalities in 46 provinces simultaneously.
  • Zero Frequency Overlap: Single 60 MHz n50 carrier covers the entire residential center without co-channel interference during market capture.
  • Rapid Market Preemption: Locks in the community with ₱599 home broadband before 4G/5G mobile operators or local WISPs can react.
  • Organic Expansion: Barangays with capacity for 2 to 4 BTS will receive secondary BTS in later phases as subscriber take-up exceeds 500 users.

Household Multipliers from DOF/PSA

Applying actual municipal household sizes (3.1 to 7.56) instead of a flat 4.4 divisor revealed critical operational realities:

  • High Household Density in Luzon: Cavite, Batangas, Laguna, Ilocos have smaller HH sizes (3.6–4.2), meaning more paying households per 1,000 population.
  • BARMM Household Reality: Sulu, Tawi-Tawi, Lanao have 5.8–7.5 people per household, resulting in fewer distinct subscription units per macro tower.
